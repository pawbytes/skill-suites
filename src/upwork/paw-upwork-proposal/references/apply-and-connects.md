# Apply & Connects

The freelancer is deciding whether a job is worth pursuing before they spend Connects on it. The outcome is an honest "should I apply" verdict grounded in fit, competition, and client quality — plus a Connects/bid recommendation. Saying *don't* apply is a real, valuable answer: Connects not wasted on a doomed job are Connects saved for a winnable one. You have their `positioning-brief.md`, `freelancer-context.md`, and `outcomes.md` in context from activation.

## What you need

The job posting, and whatever client/competition signal the freelancer can see on it — number of proposals already submitted, client's hire rate and total spend, verified payment, member-since, interviewing count. They'll have to read these off the Upwork page; ask for what they can see.

## The verdict: fit × competition × client quality

Weigh three things and give a clear recommendation, not a hedge:

- **Fit.** Does this job sit inside the freelancer's `positioning-brief.md` lane, or is it a stretch? A sharp fit in their niche is worth far more than a marginal fit they'd have to oversell. A job outside the lane is usually a pass — chasing it dilutes the positioning and competes from weakness.
- **Competition.** How many proposals are already in, and how strong is the likely field? Many proposals + a generic ask = race to the bottom. Few proposals + a specific niche need = a real opening. Early application matters; a job with 50 proposals is usually cold.
- **Client quality.** Payment verified? Decent hire rate and spend history? Reasonable budget for the ask? Or red flags — no payment verified, "expert for $5", vague scope, a history of low-paid hires, or signs they churn freelancers. A great-fit job for a bad client is still a bad bet.

Land on one of: **strong apply** (fits the lane, winnable, good client — move fast), **apply selectively** (worth it only with a sharp tailored proposal and maybe a Connects boost), or **skip** (wrong lane, cold, or red-flag client — name why and point them at better-fit jobs). Tie the read to `outcomes.md` when there's signal: if similar jobs have gone silent, factor that in.

## Connects & bid strategy

Upwork's bidding economy rewards discipline, not volume. Advise:

- **Spend where you can win.** Connects are finite; concentrate them on strong-fit, low-competition, good-client jobs rather than spraying. One tailored proposal on the right job beats ten generic ones.
- **Boosting.** A Connects boost can be worth it on a high-value, strong-fit job where landing in the client's first few views matters — not on a long-shot. Recommend boosting only when fit and client quality justify the spend.
- **Pacing.** If the freelancer is low on Connects, prioritize ruthlessly by the verdict above. If they're applying to everything, that's the problem — fewer, sharper proposals win more and cost less.
- **Rate framing.** Pull the recommended range from `positioning-brief.md` (Rate Range). Advise whether this specific job's budget supports their rate or is a low-baller to skip.

## After the verdict

If it's an apply, offer to write the proposal now (route to `proposal-craft.md`). If it's a skip, that's a complete answer — don't write a proposal for a job you just advised against. Append a `[proposal]` line to today's `daily/` log if the freelancer wants the decision recorded.
