# Client Handling

The proposal worked — a client replied. The outcome is the freelancer knowing exactly what to say next to convert a reply into a booked job: intro-call prep, the smart scope and clarifying questions to ask, and how to handle the common reply patterns without fumbling the moment. This is what carries the freelancer past the proposal into actually landing the work. You have their `positioning-brief.md`, `freelancer-context.md`, and `kit.md` in context from activation.

## What you need

What the client said — paste their reply or describe it — and which job it's about. The right next move depends entirely on what the client actually asked: a "tell me more" is different from "what's your rate" is different from "can you start Monday".

## Read the reply, then respond

Match the response to what the reply actually is:

- **"Tell me more / interested" (warm but vague).** Don't dump everything. Ask one or two sharp clarifying questions that show you understand the problem, and propose a quick call to scope it. Momentum comes from making the next step easy.
- **"What's your rate / timeline / availability".** Answer directly and confidently — pull the rate range from `positioning-brief.md`. Frame the rate against the outcome, not as a number to apologize for. Never undercut to seem eager; it signals low confidence.
- **"Can you do X / here's more detail".** Confirm you can, reflect their need back in their words, and surface any scope question that protects both sides before committing.
- **Invitation to a call/interview.** Move to intro-call prep below.

Keep the freelancer's voice from `freelancer-context.md`. Warm, competent, low-friction — the client should feel the same "this person gets me" they felt in the proposal hook.

## Intro-call prep

When a call is on, prep the freelancer to lead it without overselling:

- **Open by understanding, not pitching.** A few questions that get the client talking about their real goal and constraints — what success looks like, what's gone wrong before, the deadline and budget reality.
- **Smart scope questions.** The ones that prevent a painful project later: what's explicitly in and out, who decides, what "done" means, dependencies they own. Frame these as the freelancer being thorough, which is itself a selling signal.
- **Surface red flags early.** Unclear decision-maker, moving scope, unrealistic budget for the ask — better caught on the call than mid-project. Give the freelancer language to probe gently.
- **Close on a next step.** End the call with a concrete next action — a scoped proposal, a start date, a small paid trial — so the lead doesn't go cold.

## Scope & clarifying questions

When the freelancer just needs the questions to ask (in chat, on a call, or in a message), generate a tight list tailored to *this* job and niche — not a generic questionnaire. The best questions prove competence by revealing the freelancer has done this before and knows where projects go wrong.

## Save what works

If a reply script or set of questions lands well, offer to save it to `kit.md` (via `kit-and-outcomes.md`) so the freelancer reuses it. If the job converts to a win, that's outcome signal — route the result into `outcomes.md` so the kit and future proposals learn from it. Append a `[proposal]` line to today's `daily/` log.
