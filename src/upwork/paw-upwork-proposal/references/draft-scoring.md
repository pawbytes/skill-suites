# Draft Scoring

The freelancer already wrote a proposal and wants to know what's weak before they spend Connects on it. The outcome is an honest score, the specific weaknesses that cost replies, and a rewrite of the parts that need it — so the next send is stronger and the freelancer learns what good looks like. You have their `positioning-brief.md`, `freelancer-context.md`, and `kit.md` in context from activation.

## What you need

Both the freelancer's draft proposal and the job posting it targets. Without the posting you can only judge the writing in the abstract; with it you can judge the thing that matters — does this proposal answer *this* client. If they only paste the draft, ask for the posting (or at least what the job is).

## Score against what wins

Judge the draft the way the client will — by skimming the first two lines, then deciding whether to read on. Grade these dimensions honestly, not generously:

- **Hook.** Do the first 1–2 lines grab the client with their problem, or open with "I'm writing to apply" / a résumé recital? This is the highest-leverage line and the most common failure.
- **Relevance to the real need.** Does it answer the pain behind the posting, mirror the client's language, and obey any screening instruction — or is it a template that could go to any job?
- **Specific outcome promise.** Is there a concrete, credible result tied to their goal, or vague enthusiasm?
- **Proof.** Is the evidence relevant and tight, or a generic credential wall?
- **CTA & length.** Is there a clear, low-friction next step? Is it tight enough to respect a client reading 40 of these?
- **Voice & positioning fit.** Does it sound like this freelancer and sell their `positioning-brief.md` lane, or generic-professional?

## Deliver the critique

Give a clear overall read (a simple strong / needs-work / would-get-deleted verdict plus a 1–10 if the freelancer wants a number), then the specific weaknesses ranked by what's costing the most replies, each with the concrete fix. Don't just flag — rewrite the worst offenders inline (especially the hook) so the freelancer sees the better version, not just a note that it's weak. End by naming the one change that matters most.

Be honest. A flattering score on a doomed proposal wastes the freelancer's Connects. The job of this critique is to make the proposal win, which sometimes means telling them to scrap and restart from `proposal-craft.md`.

## Optional scorecard

If the freelancer wants a polished, shareable artifact (or is scoring several drafts), offer to write a self-contained HTML scorecard to `{project-root}/.pawbytes/upwork-suites/freelancers/{slug}/proposals/{job-slug}-score.html` — dimension scores with bars, the ranked weaknesses, and the rewritten hook — as a single file with inline styles that opens in any browser. This is optional; the inline critique is the default and usually enough.

## Close the loop

If the scoring produces a noticeably better version, offer to save the winning pattern to `kit.md` (via `kit-and-outcomes.md`). Append a `[proposal]` line to today's `daily/` log noting the draft was scored.
