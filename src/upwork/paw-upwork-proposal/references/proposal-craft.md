# Proposal Craft

The primary path and the heart of the skill. The outcome is a tailored, ready-to-send Upwork proposal that wins the interview, plus 1–2 variations to test and the reasoning that teaches the freelancer the craft. You have the freelancer's `positioning-brief.md`, `freelancer-context.md`, and `kit.md` (if they exist) already in context from activation, so you know the niche, voice, and proven angles. What you need now is the job posting.

## Decode the posting first

Never write before you understand what the client is really hiring for. Read the pasted posting for:

- **The real need.** What pain made them post this? The literal task ("build a landing page") sits on top of a deeper want ("launch my product this month and not look amateur"). Answer the deeper one.
- **The client's language.** Note the exact words and phrases they use for their problem and goal. Mirroring their language signals "I get you" faster than any credential.
- **Red flags.** Vague scope, "rockstar/ninja for cheap", unrealistic timeline, ten unrelated skills in one post, a budget that doesn't match the ask, signs they've burned prior freelancers. Flag these to the freelancer — they shape both whether to apply and how to write.
- **Budget & client signals.** Stated budget, hourly vs fixed, "experts only", verified payment, hire rate and history if the freelancer pasted it. These calibrate tone and rate framing.
- **What they'll screen on.** Many posts ask a specific question or want a specific first line ("start your proposal with the word 'banana'"). Catch these — ignoring a screening instruction is an instant reject, so the proposal must obey it up front.

Reflect a short decode back to the freelancer before writing — "here's what I think this client actually wants and the two red flags I'd watch" — so they confirm or correct your read. A misread decode produces a confident, wrong proposal. (On the headless / skip-to-draft path, skip the confirmation pause and put the decode summary inline in the saved proposal instead, so the read stays auditable.)

## Write the proposal

Build from the decode, the brief, and the kit. A proposal that wins the skim has a clear shape, though never a rigid template:

- **Hook (first 1–2 lines).** Open on the client's problem and your specific angle, in their words. This is the only part guaranteed to be read in the preview, so it carries the most weight. Cut every "I'm writing to apply" and "I came across your job posting" — they waste the hook.
- **Proof you can solve it.** One or two specific, relevant pieces of evidence — a comparable result, a sharp observation about their problem, a relevant approach. Pull case-study blurbs from `kit.md` when they fit. Specific and short beats a wall of credentials.
- **A specific outcome promise.** Name the result you'll deliver, concretely. Tie it to their pain. This is what separates a proposal from a brochure.
- **A light plan or first step.** A sentence or two showing you've already thought about *how*, which proves competence without giving away the whole consultation.
- **A clear, low-friction CTA.** Invite the next step — a quick call, a clarifying question that shows engagement. End by handing the client an easy "yes".

Keep it tight. Clients reading 40 proposals reward the one that respects their time. Write in the freelancer's voice from `freelancer-context.md`, aligned to the `positioning-brief.md` lane — not a generic professional register. Obey any screening instruction from the posting at the very top.

## Variations

Produce 1–2 variations worth testing — usually a different hook angle (problem-led vs outcome-led, or formal vs warm) — so the freelancer can A/B across similar jobs and feed results back through `kit-and-outcomes.md`. Don't pad with near-identical variants; each should test a real, different bet.

## Teach as you go

After the draft, name briefly *why* the key moves work — why that hook, why that proof, what the CTA is doing. Two or three sharp notes, not a lecture. This is the enlighten promise: the freelancer should leave able to write a stronger proposal on the next job without you.

## Save it

Write the proposal to `{project-root}/.pawbytes/upwork-suites/freelancers/{slug}/proposals/{job-slug}.md` (a short slug from the job title/client). Include the decode summary and variations in the file so it's a complete record. Offer to save a strong reusable intro or snippet to `kit.md` via `kit-and-outcomes.md`. Update `index.md` and append a `[proposal]` line to today's `daily/` log.

If `kit.md` already holds a proven angle for this niche (from `outcomes.md` feedback), lean on it — that's the compounding payoff of the kit.
